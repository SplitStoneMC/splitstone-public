<h2 id="analysis">What a replay shows</h2>
<p>Replays focus on where you lost time and why, the way top players review runs.</p>
<h3>Split analysis</h3>
<ul>
<li>A split-by-split table of time gained and lost against your opponent, with a jump to that moment.</li>
<li>Finer Nether splits: bastion leave, fortress and second portal.</li>
<li>Your averages by seed and bastion type, best splits and sum of best.</li>
<li>A comparison with your rank's average and the next rank's, with a guide built from your biggest losses.</li>
<li>Where and how you die or reset, by split.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Inventory over time</h3>
<ul>
<li>Your resources next to your opponent's: rods, pearls, logs, beds and food.</li>
<li>Decision checks such as pearls against a target, blaze rate and backups.</li>
</ul>
<h3>The full replay</h3>
<ul>
<li>Both routes drawn over the same seed.</li>
<li>Frame-by-frame and slow playback.</li>
<li>Notes and links to a moment.</li>
<li>Jumping back while you watch live.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="kept">Which matches are saved</h2>
<ul>
<li>Splitstone's server saves a ranked 1v1 or 2v2, weekly cup or Major match for good, both sides, when <b>every player in it is in the top 100</b>.</li>
<li>Nothing else is saved on the server. Game modes, co-op and private rooms never are.</li>
<li>Your own game keeps its own copies of your own matches.</li>
<li>Anyone can download a saved top-100 match in the game, from the match on a player's profile or in the match history. The website has no replay downloads.</li>
</ul>
<p>Your match history itself stays for good.</p>
<h2 id="watch">Watching live</h2>
<p class="livec" id="livec" hidden><i aria-hidden="true"></i><span><b>0</b><span class="lt"> ranked matches running now.</span> <a href="../live.html">Watch on the Live page &rarr;</a></span></p>
<ul>
<li>Ranked matches are shown live with no delay. Ghosting, using a live view of a match to cheat, is handled by the rules and reports.</li>
<li>Find a match three ways: a friend's match, the live list of top matches, or a search by player name.</li>
<li>The live list shows events first while they run, then friends' matches, then ranked matches by the players' combined Elo, and marks players who are streaming. The website's <a href="../live.html">Live page</a> shows the same order without the friends part.</li>
<li>You follow the player you came for. From the live list, you follow whoever is ahead.</li>
<li>You can't watch a match while your own run is going. Once it's over, such as after a forfeit, you may watch your opponent.</li>
<li>Live watching and replays show finer Nether splits and inventory counts, to players as well as spectators. Players don't see them on their own screen during their match.</li>
<li>Everyone chooses who can watch their matches: everyone, friends or nobody.</li>
<li>Spectators can't send messages to the player they watch.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="coaching">Coaching</h2>
<ul>
<li>A player, or a 2v2 or co-op team, can have up to two coaches in a match. You add a coach by typing their name into a search bar and pressing Add, as when adding a friend.</li>
<li>In ranked, a coach sees only their own player or team, never the opponent, since both sides play the same seed.</li>
<li>A coach can talk to their player during the match. In 2v2 and co-op, the coach talks to one teammate, chosen by the team.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="broadcast">Broadcast tools</h2>
<p>Organisers approved for an event get broadcast tools: camera control, split and timeline screens, and a live data feed for stream overlays.</p>
